An endless drum ensemble for dancing, generated in your browser. The page sends nothing anywhere and keeps working offline once loaded.

The sounds

  • Djembes, dunun and iron bells: adapted from the circAfrique v4 drumkit by Jim Harney (www.circAfrique.com), licensed CC BY-SA 3.0. Changes made: each stroke turned into ten variations (small shifts of pitch, brightness, attack and decay), softer and darker versions for quiet strokes, more low end in the bass strokes, levels rebalanced. A synthesized kick and sub-bass sound under the big drum. The resulting sound file (kit/kit.ogg) is shared under CC BY-SA 3.0 as well.
  • Shekere: Shekere by jonopodmore, CC0 1.0.

The rhythms

Almost all of these rhythms are traditional and belong to the peoples and drummers who carry them: the Malinke and neighbouring peoples of Guinea and Mali, the Ga and Ewe of Ghana, and Afro-Cuban and Afro-Brazilian traditions. A few were invented for this ensemble. What plays here is a machine's approximation, working from teaching notation, sometimes adapted to these instruments; it may contain mistakes.

The notation came from:

  • Paul Nas's West African Percussion pages, compiled from Mamady Keïta, Famoudou Konaté and other teachers
  • the Djembe-L rhythm collection
  • Michael Pluznick's free tabs
  • Larry Morris's rhythm catalogue
  • the free scores at afro-percussion.ch (Markus Maggiori; arrangements partly by B. Konaté)
  • C.K. Ladzekpo's song books (Gahu, Kpanlogo) and Mark Powers
  • the Cuban Rhythm blog
  • studies of maracatu published by ANPPOM and SIMPOM

The way the pulses are spaced and the players sit against each other follows the measurements of Rainer Polak, Justin London and Nori Jacoby (for example Polak 2010 and Jacoby, Polak & London 2021).
